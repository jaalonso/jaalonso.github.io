    Review of «Towards a world mathematical commons»

    José A. Alonso

    23-10-2026

    Review

    In his article «Towards a world mathematical commons», Kunal Marwaha observes that mathematics is a deeply creative and emotional pursuit that has outgrown individual mastery. As machines increasingly prove theorems and generate formal documents, many researchers find this shift daunting rather than liberating.

    Marwaha advocates for a paradigm shift: from solitary frontier exploration to collective stewardship of a global mathematical commons. He outlines a concrete roadmap—establishing a broad coalition of mathematicians, leveraging LLMs to formalize knowledge in Lean, and structuring the resulting verification graph into accessible, modular components.

    Under responsible governance, this infrastructure could streamline complex theories, illuminate elegant proof pathways for broader audiences, and nurture future generations working alongside AI. Marwaha concludes with an urgent call to action: the time to build this coalition is now.

    Review of «Disgusted by AI?»

    José A. Alonso

    23-10-2026

    Review

    In the article Disgusted by AI?, Marcus Zibrowius examines the resistance that much of the mathematical community feels toward the sudden arrival of AI. Initially, this rejection appears to be driven by idealistic concerns: dismay at proofs that are verified yet inexplicable, and nostalgia for an era when mathematics was grounded in beauty, personal growth, and shared understanding.

    However, the author suggests that the underlying reason is more prosaic: AI has revealed that contemporary mathematical culture rewards neither elegance nor understanding, but rather the primacy of the result—that is, who arrives first. The panic stems not from a loss of values, but from the realization that AI is more efficient than humans at playing by the discipline’s actual, competitive rules.

    Review of «Mathematics of the future»

    José A. Alonso

    22-10-2026

    Review

    The article Mathematics of the future analyzes the transformative impact of artificial intelligence on mathematics. Its authors argue that, although humans will remain indispensable for asking relevant questions and interpreting results, the profession will have to evolve profoundly to remain relevant in this new technological landscape.

    The central proposal is to replace the current culture, based on authorship and individual merit, with a system that values collective intelligence above all. To achieve this, they propose reforming both hiring processes and educational models, prioritizing broad knowledge and direct dialogue over publication lists and early specialization.

    Finally, the text advocates creating collaborative labs and recommends that young mathematicians cultivate flexibility and teamwork. The ultimate goal is to integrate AI as a tool for exploring broader mathematical horizons, without losing sight of the innate curiosity and genuine pleasure that characterize human discovery.

    Review of «Mathematics is a subject for the young»

    José A. Alonso

    22-10-2026

    Review

    In the article «Mathematics is a subject for the young», Tristan Buckmaster analyses how artificial intelligence can transform the practice of mathematics. In his view, the discipline should no longer focus on the race to solve open problems, but should instead turn towards deep understanding and its integration with the physical sciences.

    The text also denounces the conflicts of interest of AI companies, which could exploit private data and unpublished work without consent. This situation calls for stricter regulation and the development of independently funded open-source models.

    Buckmaster likewise proposes a new system of academic recognition: instead of rewarding speed or the prestige of journals, credit should be based on the quality of exposition, collaboration, and the development of new methods.

    The author concludes by expressing his amazement at what postdoctoral fellows are already achieving with these tools and, recalling the saying that mathematics belongs to the young, argues that they will be the ones to decide the future of the discipline.

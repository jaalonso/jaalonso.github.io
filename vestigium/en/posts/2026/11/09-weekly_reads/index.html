    Weekly reads: Nov 2 – Nov 9, 2026

    José A. Alonso

    09-11-2026

    Also available in:

    Español

    Here are the 34 reads I shared on Mastodon this past week.

    This week's main thread is, once again, mathematics in the age of AI. Four reads come from the Proofs and Prompts series: Filippo A. E. Nuccio on formalisation as a manifestation of mathematical understanding, Nihar Gargava on why this is a great time to be an amateur mathematician, Isabelle Gallagher on slow maths, and Beckett Bradford on what AI does for olympiad students. Terence Tao's blog hosts two further contributions: "Mathematics has changed. Let's act like it", by Frank Calegari, and "Egoless mathematics in the AI era", by Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai and Jie Xue. Two videos complete the thread: "Mathematicians in the time of AI", by Yu Deng, and "Math 2.0", by Tao himself.

    The Lean 4 section mixes formalization projects with applications. The Stacks Project has been formalized in Lean, and TechCrunch reports on Axiom Math's aim of keeping AI slop out of mathematics. Among the applications are automatic certified differentiation, the Henstock-Kurzweil path integral and operator Wick calculus, the genetic code, a two-colour zig-zag numberlink problem, and KAPPA, which checks the proofs in statistics papers with Lean 4 and language model agents. There are also new results in spectral graph theory and on Kaplansky's conjectures.

    The remaining technical reads cover other proof assistants: five Isabelle/HOL entries from the Archive of Formal Proofs (including Paulson's Abel-Ruffini theorem), verified catenable deques in Rocq, and ProofStudio, which brings five proof assistants to the browser. In the Math section, Voevodsky explains the origins of univalent foundations, and Gaussianos covers the 200 TB proof of the Pythagorean triples problem.

    The final section collects my own content: the Lean 4 challenges.

    Weekly reads: Oct 26 – Nov 1, 2026

    José A. Alonso

    02-11-2026

    Also available in:

    Español

    Here are the 27 reads I shared on Mastodon this past week.

    This week's main thread is, once again, the future of mathematics in the age of AI, now with two community reports: "The mathematical sciences in an AI-enabled world", by Christoph Reisinger et al., and the report of the "Summit on math research in the age of AI". The Proofs and Prompts series contributes Tim Gehrunger on AI reviews, Patrick Morris on whether we should stop writing papers and Alonso Castillo-Ramirez on AI and the correction of the mathematical literature. Terence Tao's blog hosts Gireeja Ranade on education and a public goods problem, Celina Zhao asks in Science whether AI proofs can be trusted, and Lauren Williams offers a nuanced assessment of AI's ability to solve math problems in a talk about First Proof.

    The Lean 4 section is dominated by large-scale formalization: Axiom has formalized the classification of finite simple groups, the longest proof in the history of mathematics. It also includes the PaperFormalizer approach to autoformalizing research papers, the degenerations of Jordan algebras and the formalization of reasoning about the genetic code.

    The Isabelle/HOL section brings five new entries to the Archive of Formal Proofs: the irrationality of ζ(5), modular forms, a countable version of Dilworth's theorem, the travelling salesman problem via dynamic programming and the consistency of NK with infinity and choice. A Spanish read on real numbers by Esteban Martínez completes the list.

    The final section collects my own content: the Lean 4 challenges on sequences and limits.
